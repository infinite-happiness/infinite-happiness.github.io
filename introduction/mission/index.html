The Mission

How Ánanda Márga transforms spiritual practice into active social service. The global welfare mission of Ánanda Márga Pracáraka Saḿgha.

Meaning

means the path of bliss. Ánanda is defined as bliss, and further explained as infinite happiness. The path of bliss is a path where you get to experience true happiness by getting to know, and live by, your true nature — dharma.

Motto

The mission, as well as motto, of is:

– Self-realization and service to the Universe.

Selfless service is vital for spiritual practice, just as spiritual practice is essential for true social service.

Self-Realization

The spiritual aspect of the mission is , or self-realization. It means to realize your fullest potential, not just as a human being, but as a divine being.

Self-realization is the ultimate goal of spirituality, which means to get to know yourself on the deepermost level of who you are.

A more literal translation of is the complete and permanent liberation of the soul. As the soul is liberated from worldly attachments, the desire for God increases. God comes closer and closer to the practitioner, through more intense spiritual practice, until there is no distinction between the personal self and God. There is no selfishness or petty ego left. Only pure divinity, complete consciousness.

Hence, the end goal of is very clear and precise. The process is also explained in very exact detail.

Self-realization is the goal of , as well as of . For , it is also the essence of all teachings, both spiritual and social.

Service

Selfless service, or , takes many forms. It encompasses everything from protecting ecosystems to providing all beings with basic needs such as food and healthcare. For humans, this extends to offering clothing, housing and education, as well as spiritual guidance and training. Innovation and art are also forms of service, as long as they are guided by welfare.

Historically, is strongly associated with , but it also holds an important place in Tantra.

According to , is understood as loving service to God. It is a vital part of spirituality that should not be neglected.

Most spiritual paths include a philosophy and practice, together with benevolence as a key feature. Yet, research indicates that the majority seldom engage in hands-on volunteering, despite what their philosophy advocates. Furthermore, the vast majority of financial donations do not fund charitable work. Instead, they normally go to running the religious organization itself.
